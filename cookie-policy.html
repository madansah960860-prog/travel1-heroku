Cookie Policy

Last updated August 12, 2026. This page lists every cookie and storage item this website can set, what each one does, how long it lasts, and how to change your mind at any point.

Change your mind right now

Open the consent panel and turn analytics or advertising on or off. Whatever you choose takes effect immediately.

1. What a cookie is

A cookie is a small text file a website asks your browser to hold onto. Later it can be read back by the site that set it, or, in the case of a third-party cookie, by some other company whose code is running on the page. We also use local storage, which behaves much the same way but is not sent along with every request. Everywhere in this policy, "cookie" means both.

2. Our consent rule

Until you choose, this site writes nothing beyond strictly necessary storage. Not one analytics cookie and not one advertising cookie is set before you press Accept all, or switch that category on inside Manage preferences and save it. Press Reject non-essential and no analytics or advertising cookie will ever reach this device unless you go back and change it yourself.

Your choice is stored in local storage under the key ftk_consent_v1 so that we do not ask you again on every page. That single item is itself strictly necessary, because without it we would have no record of what you decided.

3. Strictly necessary

Always on. The site will not run without them and there is no opt out, though your browser settings can block them, at the price of breaking bookings and orders.

Third-party vendors, including Google, use cookies to serve advertisements based on your prior visits to this website or other websites. You can opt out of personalised advertising at Google Ads Settings, at aboutads.info, or at the Network Advertising Initiative opt-out. Declining advertising in our banner stops them being set here in the first place, which is the simpler route.

6. Third-party content we keep off this site

There are no social media widgets embedded here, no comment systems, no chat popups, no session recorders and no heat mapping tools. Fonts come from Google Fonts, which sees your IP address as part of serving the file but sets no cookie for it. Photographs come from image hosts, which likewise see your IP address in the course of delivering the picture.

7. Controlling cookies in your browser

Every major browser will let you view, block and delete cookies. Block them all and reservations and ordering break here, as they will on most sites. The settings pages worth knowing:

  • Chrome: Settings, then Privacy and security, then Third-party cookies
  • Safari: Settings, then Privacy
  • Firefox: Settings, then Privacy and Security
  • Edge: Settings, then Cookies and site permissions

The Global Privacy Control signal is honoured here. If your browser or an extension sends one, we read it as an instruction to switch advertising off, on top of whatever you selected in our banner.

8. Changes

Add a vendor or a cookie and this table changes, with the date at the top moving to match. Where a whole new category appears, consent gets asked for again rather than assumed from your last answer.
