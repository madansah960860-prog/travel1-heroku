The business, not the brochure

About Embervine

Embervine LLC is a privately held restaurant company registered in North Carolina, running three restaurants, one catering kitchen and a small retail line. This page sets out where the money comes from and how the people working here are treated.

What this company actually is

One state, no outside investors, no franchise arrangements, and no intention of taking on either. Ownership sits with Marisol Vandergriff and Desmond Achebe, alongside a 12 percent employee ownership pool that vests at three years of service.

There are 118 people on the books across the three restaurants and the catering kitchen. About two thirds are full time. Anyone past 20 hours a week carries health coverage, and the starting wage is published rather than left for applicants to prise out of us.

This is not a chain and there is no ambition to turn it into one. Three restaurants is about as far as a single chef can genuinely reach, and we treat that as the ceiling.

Six revenue lines, and the job each one does

A restaurant living on its dining room alone gets one bad winter and shuts. Here is where the money genuinely comes from, and the price on each line.

1. Dine-in service

The spine of the business, around 58 percent of revenue. Three booking tiers: standard tables free, the Garden Table on a $25 deposit credited back to your check, and the Chef's Table tasting at $145.

Reservation tiers

2. Takeout and delivery

Around 16 percent. Ordered direct, you pay menu price with no service fee. Our own delivery runs a flat $4.95 inside five miles. The third-party apps add a markup of their own, which is not ours to set.

How ordering works

3. Catering and private events

Around 15 percent, and easily the most seasonal line. Drop-off from $28 a guest, staffed events $65 to $110 a guest, and the private dining room from a $500 room fee.

Package pricing

4. Gift cards

Around 6 percent, and heavily stacked into November and December. Cards run $25 to $500, never expire, and carry no dormancy or inactivity fee whatsoever.

Buy or check a card

5. The Twelve Mile Club

Free to join, and not a revenue line of its own, but it is the reason roughly a third of our covers are returning guests. A point a dollar on food and drink, with 250 points turning into a $25 credit. No tiers, nothing expiring, no app to install.

Join at any host stand

6. Pantry retail

Around 5 percent. Chili crisp, sorghum vinegar, house coffee and a canvas tote, priced at what they cost to make properly plus an ordinary retail margin.

How the people here get paid

Front of house opens at $18 an hour before tips, kitchen at $21. Both sit above the applicable state minimum in all three states we trade in, and no tip credit is taken against wages.

In 2025 the 3 percent kitchen appreciation fee that used to sit at the foot of every check came off, and menu prices went up to cover it. What you actually pay barely moved. What did move is that the real price is now visible before you order, which is where it should have been all along.

  • No automatic service charge on any check
  • No card processing surcharge passed to guests
  • Tips are pooled and distributed to the whole shift, kitchen included
  • Health coverage from 20 hours a week

The things we will not do

Being clear about the limits saves everyone time.

  • We will not put a dish on the card that cannot be sourced locally in that season
  • We will not add a fee to a check that was not visible before you ordered
  • We will not run fake countdowns, invented scarcity or made-up review quotes anywhere on this site
  • We will not sell or share guest personal information with data brokers, and you can see exactly what we do collect in the Privacy Policy
  • We will not open a fourth restaurant while three still need this much attention
