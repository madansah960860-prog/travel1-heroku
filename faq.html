The questions we actually get

Twenty-two answers spanning booking, money, food, access and the things guests genuinely ask the host stand. If yours is not among them, the contact page will reach a person.

Booking and tables

Your table is held fifteen minutes past the booking time without anyone asking, and a good deal longer if you ring to say you are delayed. Past fifteen minutes with no word, it may pass to waiting guests, though we will always work to seat you again the moment something frees up.

What it costs

Sales tax goes on at the applicable state and local rate, and it is the only line on your check you did not order yourself.

The 3 percent kitchen fee came off in 2025 and menu prices went up to cover it, because a surprise line on a check has never been a wage policy.

Every major credit and debit card, contactless, cash, and Embervine gift cards. No surcharge for paying by card. Splitting a check is fine, up to six ways on one table.

Food, allergens and diets

Most dishes can be adapted, and every line on the card carries GF, V, VG and N tags. What we will not do is promise an allergen free plate. Wheat, dairy, eggs, tree nuts, peanuts, soy, fish, shellfish and sesame all move through one shared space on shared equipment. Tell your server before ordering rather than after, and it goes straight through to the kitchen instead of stopping at the host stand. Read the full allergen and nutrition disclaimer.

Yes, and not bolted on at the end. On the current card nine of twenty-six dishes are vegetarian as written and five are vegan as written, one of them a main off the hearth rather than only starters and sides. The fire roasted cabbage outsells every other vegetarian main by a wide margin.

Because we buy inside a twelve mile radius and write the card only once Tuesday’s produce is through the door. Roughly a third of it turns over weekly. If something you loved has gone, ask a server. Sometimes the gap is two weeks and sometimes the season is finished until next year, and you will get told which rather than fobbed off.

There is a children’s plate at $9, which is a smaller serving of real food off the main card rather than a separate list of fried things. High chairs and booster seats are on hand in all three rooms. Children are welcome at every service, the private room included, though we would gently point you away from the Chef’s Table, which runs two and a half hours.

The sungold tomato toast softens inside ten minutes, the skillet cornbread surrenders its crust, and the shellfish pot is honestly not worth ordering to go. You will hear that at the point of ordering rather than after we have taken the money. Everything off the hearth, the grits and all four puddings travel perfectly well.

Getting here, and getting in

Asheville: the Rankin Avenue garage sits a four minute walk away at about $2 an hour, with two reserved takeout bays right outside the door. Charleston: the Queen Street garage two blocks south, plus free street meters from 6:00 PM. Nashville: a free surface lot behind the building holding 22 spaces, two of them accessible.

All three offer step-free entry and an accessible restroom. Nashville adds a ramped rear entrance and an automatic restroom door. Asheville’s private room sits upstairs behind stairs only, so private bookings move to a downstairs configuration on request at no extra charge. Detail room by room is on the locations page, and the accessibility statement covers this website.

None at all, in any of the three. One service will bring people straight off a trail and people straight out of an office, and neither is dressed wrong.

Dogs are welcome on the Charleston courtyard between March and November, and at the Asheville sidewalk tables. Indoors is service animals only, and those are welcome absolutely everywhere. There are water bowls at every host stand.
