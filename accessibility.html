Our accessibility statement

Last updated August 12, 2026. Our aim is to meet Web Content Accessibility Guidelines 2.1 at Level AA right across this site, and to keep all three rooms usable by every guest who walks, rolls or is guided through the door.

1. What we are committing to

Accessibility here is not a box ticked once and filed. It sits inside how this site is built and how the rooms are run. Our position is that a guest should not have to ask for special treatment to read a menu, book a table or get to a restroom.

This site is built to conform with WCAG 2.1 Level AA. We judge it substantially conformant, subject to the known exceptions set out in section 4.

2. What is already in place on this site

  • Semantic structure. Every page carries proper landmarks, a banner, a navigation region, a main region and a footer, with headings running in logical order and no level skipped.
  • Skip link. On every page the first stop for keyboard focus is a "Skip to main content" link.
  • Keyboard operation. Every control works from the keyboard alone, the mobile menu, the dropdowns, the FAQ accordions, the gallery filter and the cookie banner included. Escape shuts anything open.
  • Visible focus. A three pixel ring in our wine accent lands on every focusable element. Default focus styling has not been stripped anywhere on this site.
  • Contrast. Body text is checked to at least 4.5 to 1 against whatever sits behind it, with large text and interface components held to at least 3 to 1. Our plum ink on blush stone measures approximately 13 to 1.
  • Images. Every photograph carries descriptive alternative text, and the decorative drawings are marked so screen readers pass straight over them.
  • Forms. Every field carries a visible label rather than a placeholder pretending to be one. Errors surface beside the field they belong to, are announced to assistive technology, and explain how to put the problem right instead of simply calling the entry invalid.
  • Motion. Animations honour the operating system "reduce motion" setting and drop to no movement whatsoever when it is switched on. Nothing here flashes, auto-plays audio, or moves unless you ask it to.
  • Zoom and reflow. The layout holds at 320 pixels wide and at 400 percent browser zoom with no sideways scrolling and nothing lost. Text takes a 200 percent resize without breaking.
  • Touch targets. Interactive controls run to at least 44 by 44 pixels, with clear space between them.
  • No traps. Nothing pops up to block the page as you arrive. The cookie banner sits along the bottom, is reachable by keyboard, and never stops you reading what is underneath it.

3. The assistive technology we test on

Pages get checked on VoiceOver across macOS and iOS, NVDA on Windows with Firefox, keyboard-only navigation in Chrome, and browser zoom pushed to 400 percent. Testing runs at 360, 768, 1024, 1440 and 1920 pixels wide.

4. Known limitations, set out honestly

These are the parts we know are not right yet. Publishing a statement that claims perfection helps nobody.

  • A few of the longer data tables, the example event quote among them, still need horizontal scrolling on very narrow screens. Each one scrolls inside its own container instead of shoving the page sideways, but it remains less comfortable than we want it to be.
  • The PDF menus we supply to third-party listing sites are not fully tagged for screen readers. The HTML card on this site is the accessible source of truth, and it is always the current one.
  • The third-party delivery platforms we link out to run their own accessibility, which is not ours to control. If one of them puts a barrier in your way, tell us and we will take the order directly instead.
  • The Asheville private dining room sits upstairs behind stairs alone. Any private booking moves to a ground floor configuration on request at no extra cost, and we would far rather you asked than assumed.

If something on this site or in a restaurant does not work for you, we want to hear about it and we will treat it as a defect rather than a complaint.
